This workflow begins from a taxonomic abundance report produced by Bracken. We perform an automated decontamination step using DESeq2 and then generate three filtered datasets at minimum abundance thresholds of 0.001, 0.01 and 0.05; these datasets are nested (the dataset with the highest threshold is contained within the lower-threshold datasets). After automatic filtering, we apply a supervised decontamination step to further refine the sample set.

For each of the three resulting RDS objects we run three differential analysis methods—MaAsLin3, limma and LEfSe—using four discriminants of interest (subtentorial lesions, gadolinium contrast, lesion burden and spinal cord lesions). Results from all methods and thresholds are combined: a feature is considered a differential abundant signature (DAS) for a given category if at least one method reports a p-value < 0.05. The merged outputs are then used to produce final DAS lists and downstream visualizations.
